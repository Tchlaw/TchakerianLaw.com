FAQ

Common questions

Does my startup need patents at all?

Not always. Many startups do best with a small number of well chosen patents, and some should wait. Shant's job is to tell you plainly which inventions are worth the cost and which are not.

When should we start filing?

Earlier than most founders expect. Most countries require filing before any public disclosure, such as a launch, demo, or published paper. The U.S. allows one year after your own disclosure. If a launch is coming up, reach out first.

What does it cost?

It depends on how you work together. A patent strategy is a fixed fee, ongoing counsel is a monthly fee, and patent application drafting is priced per application. Fees are agreed in writing before any work begins.

Can you work with the patent firm we already use?

Yes. Shant can manage your existing firm, review its drafts with your engineers, and oversee filing, so your team has one person accountable for the result.

How much engineering time does this take?

Usually a few short conversations with the engineers who built each feature. Shant prepares the questions and handles the writing, so your team stays focused on shipping.

Do you work with startups outside California?

Yes. Patent applications are filed with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office, a federal agency, so Shant can work with startups across the country.
